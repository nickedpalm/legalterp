Finding a certified court interpreter in [Portland](/locations/oregon/portland/) shouldn't take three phone calls, a referral chain, and a prayer — but for anyone who's tried to staff a last-minute arraignment or a multi-day immigration hearing, that's usually how it goes. Portland's legal market has grown faster than its interpreter registry, and the gap between "someone who speaks Spanish" and "an FCICE-certified interpreter with real courtroom experience" is exactly where depositions go sideways. This directory exists to close that gap.

## How to Choose a Certified Court Interpreter in Portland

- **Verify the credential, not just the language claim.** Oregon state courts recognize NCSC State Court Certified interpreters — for federal proceedings, FCICE certification is the bar. For immigration hearings at Portland's Executive Office for Immigration Review (EOIR), look specifically for DOJ EOIR Accreditation. "Bilingual" and "certified" are not synonyms.

- **Match the interpreter to the proceeding type.** A simultaneous interpreter suited for trial testimony operates differently than a consecutive interpreter who's spent years on client consultations. Ask directly: how many depositions have you interpreted? How many federal court appearances?

- **Check language pair coverage, not just the primary language.** Portland has significant communities speaking Spanish, Russian, Somali, Vietnamese, and Cantonese — but certified interpreter availability drops sharply outside Spanish. If your matter involves a less common language pair, start the search earlier than you think you need to.

- **Confirm availability and conflict of interest in one call.** Oregon's legal community is smaller than it looks. An interpreter who's worked for opposing counsel or knows a key witness needs to disclose it. Build this into your vetting call from the start.

- **Ask about remote vs. in-person availability explicitly.** Many Portland interpreters now handle Zoom depositions and remote hearings, but simultaneous interpretation over video requires specific equipment and setup. Don't assume — confirm their remote protocol before booking.

> **Pro Tip:** For Multnomah County Circuit Court proceedings, check whether the interpreter appears on the Oregon Judicial Department's approved interpreter roster. OJD maintains its own list of court-certified and registered interpreters — and some judges will specifically ask about it.

## What to Expect

Portland court interpreters typically bill $350–$750 per assignment, with half-day minimums common for depositions and full-day rates applying to trial testimony. Turnaround for routine scheduling is 48–72 hours for common language pairs; rare language pairs — Somali, Tigrinya, less common Asian languages — may require a week or more of lead time. Most certified interpreters require written confirmation and will ask for case materials in advance for technical or specialized proceedings.

> **Reality Check:** The cheapest quote often means either no certification or double-booking risk — two situations that can compromise interpreted testimony's admissibility. In federal proceedings, using an uncertified interpreter when a certified one was available has resulted in mistrial motions. Pay the rate for the credential.

## Local Market Overview

Portland sits at the intersection of a growing immigrant legal services sector and a federal court district that handles a significant immigration docket — meaning demand for Spanish, Russian, and Somali interpreters regularly outpaces the certified pool, especially during peak court seasons in spring and fall. The Multnomah County legal market also draws matters from surrounding counties, so factor in travel time when the proceeding is outside the city proper.
