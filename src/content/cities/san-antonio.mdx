Finding a certified court interpreter in [San Antonio](/locations/texas/san-antonio/) shouldn't feel like a coin flip, but for too many attorneys it does — you call around, get three referrals from the same agency, and end up paying premium rates for someone whose only "certification" is a language school certificate from 2009. San Antonio's bilingual population is one of the largest in the country, which means the interpreter market is crowded with people who speak Spanish fluently and assume that's enough. It isn't.

> **The Short Version:** Verify FCICE or NCSC certification before booking anyone for federal court or high-stakes depositions. For state proceedings, Texas ONCOR-certified interpreters are the minimum standard. Below, I break down how to vet, what to pay, and what the local market actually looks like.

## How to Choose a Certified Court Interpreter in San Antonio

- **Check the credential, not the résumé.** FCICE (Federal Court Interpreter Certification Examination) is the gold standard for federal proceedings — fewer than 1,500 interpreters hold it nationwide. For Bexar County state court work, look for ONCOR certification through the Texas Judicial Branch. Ask for the certificate number and verify it directly. Fluency is not a credential.

- **Match the certification to the assignment.** An EOIR-accredited interpreter is required for immigration court (CPS hearings, removal proceedings at the San Antonio Immigration Court on Loop 410). RID CI certification matters if you're working with deaf witnesses or parties. Don't use a deposition interpreter for an immigration bond hearing just because they're available.

- **Ask about simultaneous vs. consecutive.** Depositions typically use consecutive interpretation (speaker pauses, interpreter follows). Trials and some hearings require simultaneous. Not every interpreter is trained in both modes — ask directly, and if they hedge, move on.

- **Confirm prior Bexar County courtroom experience.** The 37 district courts in Bexar County have their own procedural rhythms. An interpreter who's worked the 225th or 288th District Court regularly will handle a tense cross-examination differently than someone whose experience is mostly document translation.

- **Get a confirmation of availability in writing.** Multi-day trials are where this falls apart. An interpreter who's available Monday may have a conflicting deposition Thursday. Lock down the full assignment calendar upfront, especially for anything running longer than a half day.

> **Pro Tip:** NAJIT's online directory lets you search by language pair and location. Cross-reference anyone you're considering against the Texas Office of Court Administration's certified interpreter roster — both take about four minutes and will immediately filter out a lot of noise.

## What to Expect

Court interpreter rates in San Antonio typically run $350–750 per assignment, with federal court and simultaneous interpretation at the higher end. Most interpreters charge a half-day minimum (2–4 hours), so a 45-minute arraignment still costs you the minimum. Rush bookings — anything under 48 hours for rare language pairs like Tigrinya or Mandarin — carry surcharges that can push costs above $900.

> **Reality Check:** The biggest pricing mistake attorneys make is shopping for the lowest hourly rate without accounting for minimums and cancellation fees. An interpreter at $95/hour with a 4-hour minimum and a 24-hour cancellation policy will cost you more on a short hearing than one at $125/hour with a 2-hour minimum and a 48-hour cancellation window. Get the full rate card before you book.

## Local Market Overview

San Antonio's legal market is driven by its position as a border-adjacent city with one of the country's busiest immigration courts, a large military JAG presence at JBSA, and a Bexar County court system processing over 100,000 cases annually — Spanish-English interpretation demand is consistently high, but don't assume availability. For less common language pairs (Vietnamese, Arabic, Somali), build in a 5–7 day lead time and have a backup agency on standby.
