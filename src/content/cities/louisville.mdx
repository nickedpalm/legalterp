## How to Choose a Certified Court Interpreter in Louisville

- **Match credentials to the proceeding type.** Federal court matters (WDKY in Louisville) require FCICE-certified interpreters for Spanish — full stop. State proceedings at Jefferson County Circuit or Family Court can use NCSC-certified interpreters, but the standard varies by judge. Immigration hearings at the Louisville immigration court require DOJ EOIR accreditation. Confirm the venue before you book, not after.
- **Verify the language pair, not just the language.** "Spanish interpreter" covers 20+ national dialects. A Guatemalan-born interpreter may struggle with the Puerto Rican Spanish of your client. For rarer pairs — Arabic, Somali, Burmese, or the significant Amharic-speaking population Louisville has built through refugee resettlement — always ask for a live sample or reference from a prior legal proceeding.
- **Ask for assignment-specific experience.** Consecutive interpretation in a two-hour deposition is a different skill set than simultaneous interpretation during a fast-moving suppression hearing. Ask for three recent comparable assignments. If they can't name them, keep looking.
- **Confirm availability for the full proceeding window.** Multi-day trials require an interpreter who can block the calendar, not just show up for day one. Get a hold-confirmation in writing, including a cancellation policy — last-minute interpreter fallout is a continuance waiting to happen.
- **Check NAJIT membership as a professionalism signal.** It's not a certification, but NAJIT members subscribe to a code of ethics and continuing education standards that credentialed-but-lapsed interpreters often don't maintain.

> **Pro Tip:** Jefferson County is one of the busiest court dockets in Kentucky. For anything scheduled in the next 2–3 weeks, contact interpreters directly rather than routing through a general agency — agency availability often reflects their call list, not actual confirmed booking.

## What to Expect

Rates for certified court interpreters in Louisville typically run $350–750 per assignment, with half-day minimums common for deposition work and full-day rates kicking in for trials. Turnaround for booking a credentialed interpreter in a common language pair like Spanish is usually 48–72 hours; rare language pairs (Somali, Burmese, Bosnian — all languages with active Louisville speaker communities) may require a week or more. Most interpreters invoice net-30 with a signed confirmation letter.

> **Reality Check:** The cheapest quote is almost never the safe quote. Uncertified interpreters routinely undercut credentialed professionals by 40–50%, and the savings evaporate the moment opposing counsel challenges interpreter qualifications mid-proceeding. Inadmissible testimony and emergency continuances cost more than the rate difference.

## Local Market Overview

Louisville's legal market is anchored by WDKY federal court and Jefferson County's high-volume circuit and family divisions, with a growing immigration docket driven by the city's status as one of the top refugee resettlement destinations in the Midwest — the International Center of Kentucky has resettled over 20,000 refugees since 1990, making language access a routine operational requirement, not an edge case. Spanish, Somali, Burmese, Arabic, and Bosnian are the highest-demand pairs, and credentialed availability in the latter three is genuinely tight.
